So C++ 26 has now finalized and becomes the latest standard for C++. There are a lot of things to be excited about. Arguably the "big 3" features of this new standard would be reflection, contracts and execution library, which focus on the language expressivity, safety and concurrency. There have been many great articles and videos online already that talks about the "big 3" features. However, I find it rather difficult to find relevant matrial on one of my most anticipated features in the new standard: The Data-Parallel Types (SIMD).

As a programmer working in the field of high frequency trading. Latency is often one of the most important and impactful metric of the trading system. While the trading servers are pretty beefy with ample number of CPU core. We often tend to avoid multi-threading operations in the critical path to minimize the context switching/resource contentions etc. Instead, we try to squeeze every laste juice in a single CPU core.
